CASP RNA SIG

A Special Interest Group for nucleic acid 3D structure prediction. We meet online every two weeks to talk about the state of the field and what is new in it, from experimental data and benchmark sets to alignments, ligands and ensembles.

About

Nucleic acid 3D structure, discussed as a community

The SIG grew out of the CASP RNA category. It brings together people who predict, determine and assess RNA and DNA structures, and gives them a regular place to compare notes between CASP rounds.

Alongside the talks we run a Google Group for announcements and longer email discussion.

The series started in May 2023, organized by Rachael Kretsch (Das and Chiu labs, Stanford) and Marcin Magnus (Rivas lab, Harvard).

Along the way they were joined by Nivedita Dutta (University of Houston), Chaitanya Joshi (Stanford University) and Elise White (Johns Hopkins University).

We are looking for more organizers; the job is to invite 2–4 speakers a year.

    Course

    A semester of RNA structure

    The recorded talks, arranged as a 15-week course for RNA aficionados. Each week pairs two talks to watch before a discussion session. Topics move from secondary structure and chemical probing to 3D structure, dynamics, interactions, evolution and design.

    Gap: no recorded talk yet on sequence alignments and covariation. That makes a good guest lecture for week 2.
